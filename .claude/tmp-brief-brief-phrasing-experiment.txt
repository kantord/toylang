# brief-phrasing-experiment

Tracked in-repo per maintainer ruling (coordinator-escalation-brief-phrasing-experiment round,
2026-09-01: `gh issue create` was blocked by the permission classifier, so this has no gh issue
and no retry attempt should be made to file one).

Maintainer note (composed 2026-08-31): generic non-informational reassurance phrasing (e.g. "it's
much simpler than you think") in dispatch briefs seems to help planning agents find better plans.

Your task:
1. Find the dispatch brief template(s) used to hand work to sandboxed/delegated build agents in
   this repo (look at `.claude/scripts/sandbox_dispatch.py` and any brief-writing conventions in
   `.claude/skills/enwiro-delegate/SKILL.md`).
2. Design a controlled experiment: add the reassurance phrasing to briefs for at least 10 issues
   (a mix of build/research rows), keep a control group without it, and record outcomes (did the
   agent converge faster, find a simpler plan, avoid over-scoping, etc.) for each.
3. Since you cannot literally dispatch 10 other lanes yourself from inside this task, instead:
   research how such an experiment would be measured (what signal in existing dispatch logs under
   `~/.cache/toylang-drive/` and `plans/` already indicates plan quality/convergence speed), then
   either (a) run a retrospective comparison over already-completed dispatch logs that happen to
   have used the phrasing vs not, or (b) write up the experiment design plus a data-collection
   mechanism (e.g. a field in the brief template plus a log line to grep for later) so future
   dispatches can be tagged and compared automatically.
4. Write findings (or the experiment-design + instrumentation, if not enough historical data
   exists yet) to `plans/`, then compose a design/decide round reporting progress and asking the
   maintainer how to proceed.

This is exploratory: it's fine if the concrete answer is "here is the instrumentation, results
need N more ticks to accumulate" rather than a final verdict.
